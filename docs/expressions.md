# Expressions

Each construction here replaces one familiar expression, e.g. `a >= b` or `x / y`, where the hard part is an exact FP32 result. Constructions that replace a loop are in [patterns.md](patterns.md), and choices that change what the user sees are in [behavior-choices.md](behavior-choices.md). `f(x)` means `Math.fround(x)`, and `[condition]` means 1 when the condition holds and 0 otherwise.

## Decision bits

A score isn't automatically a decision. A rectangle edge may produce `0.3`, which is useful for smooth math but invalid for `open`, `focused` or `maximized`: passing it onward as state would give a partly maximized window. Make the bit exact at the moment a score becomes a decision, e.g. click ownership, focus or visibility flags, and keep positions, sizes, opacity, scale and springs at full precision. Once a bit exists, products of bits, and sums of bits that can't be 1 at the same time, stay exact bits, so the constructions below are needed only where a score first becomes a decision.

`emitCompare(circuit, a, relation, b)` in numeric-ui-core.ts is the default. It picks one of the forms below from both operands' declared ranges, which must be finite, e.g. an integer bit in 2 operations and 2 layers when both operands are declared on a grid and every partial sum stays within 2^24 grid steps, or a sign cascade in 15 operations and 14 layers for `a >= b` within 0..16,384. [execution-model.md](execution-model.md#comparisons) has the full cost table. Integer bits and a sign cascade return the same bits, so a comparison that became a sign cascade doesn't show in any result: count `circuit.roundingPoints()` by kind after building, e.g. comparing an index read within 0..16,384 without a declared grid adds 15 `'sign cascade'` entries, and `circuit.assumeGrid(read, 1)` before the comparison gives 2 `'integer bit'` entries instead ([rounding points](execution-model.md#rounding-points)). The three forms, from cheapest to most general:

- **Integer bits.** When the score is an integer count, `relu(score) - relu(score - 1)` is already the exact bit of `score >= 1`, since an integer can't land strictly between 0 and 1, e.g. `visible = clamp01(baseVisible + how many selected slots reveal me)`. This form needs no special constant, trains with the plain reference backward pass, since for a fractional score inside 0..1 the same expression is the identity ramp, and stays exact in narrower formats like bfloat16 as long as the integers fit the mantissa (validated in [lab/event-window/integer-bits.test.ts](../lab/event-window/integer-bits.test.ts)). On a grid, `emitCompare` builds `a >= b` as `relu(1 - relu(-D))` on the integer `D = (a - b) / grid`.
- **The boolean atom.** When the score is continuous and lands in 0..1, e.g. a clamped hit distance, adding 8,388,609 and then subtracting it rounds the score at 0.5. The atom is one operation, total on 0..1, but FP32-specific: the executor has to keep the stored FP32 evaluation, and `backpropagateReference` throws on the atom unless the caller passes `{booleanGradient: 'straight-through'}`. `emitCompare` ends with an atom when passed `{endWithBoolean: true}`.
- **The sign cascade.** When the decision compares two continuous values and must be exact for any finite difference, keep `d = f(a - b)` as its own operation, which is 0 exactly when `a === b` and otherwise has the sign of `a - b`. Then alternate multiplying its positive part by a power of two (exact in FP32: the exponent shifts and the mantissa is untouched) with clamping, until every positive `d`, down to 2^-149, has reached at least 1, so a last ReLU product, e.g. `relu(1 - lifted)`, is an exact bit. Every stage computes an exact value, so the bit stays right when an executor evaluates a stage in float64 or adds its terms in another order, as long as each stage stays its own operation. This came out of the [trace compiler](../lab/trace-compiler/README.md#the-sign-cascade), whose compiled circuits contain zero boolean atoms, so the 8,388,609 constant is a one-operation shortcut for this construction, not a necessity.

An integer bit written as `relu(score) - relu(score - 1)` can stop being exact inside a sum that reads it. The bit is an ordinary affine sum of 2 ReLU terms, and the builder copies an ordinary affine sum of fewer than 4 terms into the sums that read it, so when those ReLUs hold large values, the reader's running FP32 sum rounds. E.g. in lists, fractional weights over ReLU values up to 8,192 made a reciprocal's starting estimate 233% wrong, and in input, a 2^18 coefficient pushed partial sums past 2^24 and arrow-key navigation picked the wrong control ([lab/ui-exploration/README.md](../lab/ui-exploration/README.md#where-it-got-awkward)). Keep such a bit as its own `roundAffine` wire. `emitCompare`'s bits end with a ReLU product or a boolean atom, which the builder never copies into a reader.

The trace compiler switched to the cascade after a rounding stage gave a different bit from the FP32 source in a real layout case: at a viewport width of 1,732 px, `f(f(1732 - 24) × f(1 / 244))` is 6.9999995, one FP32 step below 7, and the July stage's score lost that difference. That stage's scale was too small: a rounding stage gives FP32 JavaScript's bit there whenever its scale is at least 2^-5, e.g. a stage planned from the declared range. Every form still differs from float64 at this width, because the reciprocal rounds before any comparison runs, while a [threshold count](../lab/ui-exploration/guide/patterns/threshold-counts.md) avoids the reciprocal. [What the 1732 px case shows](../lab/ui-exploration/guide/sections/comparisons.md#what-the-1732-px-case-shows) has the measurements.

History: the relu form is the oldest. The first commit's integer machinery had it as `emitStepForInteger`, and it was deleted with the move to continuous coordinates (`6cf0d81`), one day before the rounding boolean arrived (`fd8f0a3`). The relu form returned once behavior decisions turned out to be integer counts after all, the sign cascade then closed the gap for continuous comparisons, and since September 14, 2026, `emitCompare` builds both.

## Magnitude safety

FP32 holds every integer exactly only up to 2^24 (16,777,216), and above that, neighboring values are 2, then 4 apart. Macros that subtract inside their expansion lose precision past that point, e.g. `clamp01(v) = relu(v) - relu(v - 1)` returns 2 at `2^24 + 2` and 0 at `2^25`, and `emitMaximum(2^24, -1)` returns 16,777,215. Keep the operands of clamps, `emitMinimum` and `emitMaximum` under 2^23 (about 8.4 million). Where a min, max or clamp must be exact for fractional operands, use `emitMinimumExact`, `emitMaximumExact` or `emitClampExact`. When a clamp's operand is a small affine sum, which the builder copies into both ReLUs, that limit holds only for multiples of 1/2, e.g. `v = x + 0.25` at `x = f(0.8)` returns 0.99999994, so keep such an operand as its own `roundAffine` wire ([execution-model.md](execution-model.md#clamping)). Pixel-space UI, with coordinates in the thousands and scores in 0..1, sits far inside this limit unless a construction scales values up on purpose, e.g. with a `× 100000` sharpening constant. `emitSelect` at a condition of exactly 0 or 1 returns the chosen finite operand exactly at any magnitude, and `emitMultiply` equals one FP32 product for finite operands. [execution-model.md](execution-model.md#macro-contracts) has every macro's contract and failing values. Declared ranges and grids are promises the builder trusts, e.g. a false grid turns an integer bit into a fraction ([declarations](execution-model.md#declarations)).

## Circular coordinates

Not built or tested yet. Represent a position in a repeating sequence as a point on a circle, e.g. item i of n at `(cos(2π i / n), sin(2π i / n))`. The first and last items are then close, so cyclic distance and direction need no modulo. For a fixed item count, the circle points are constants, and the remaining math is differentiable.

## Dynamic division

The idea: use FP32's changing precision as the nonlinearity that computes the reciprocal of a runtime value, then multiply by the numerator, so dividing two runtime values needs no new instruction. That idea is still untested. Two other constructions divide by a runtime value, and neither is in numeric-ui-core.ts:

- lab/ui-exploration/numeric-library's `emitReciprocal(x)` reads a starting estimate from threshold bits, one per octave of the divisor's declared range, then runs Newton steps `e × relu(2 - x × e)`, which square the relative error at each step. Over 1..16,384 it costs 50 operations in 13 layers, with relative error at most 2.5e-7, and `emitDivide` costs 51 in 14. The divisor needs a declared range low..high with low > 0 and high / low <= 2^22 ([numeric-library](../lab/ui-exploration/numeric-library/README.md#contracts)).
- lab/ui-exploration/codegen's life-calendar computes `Math.floor(week / m)` for a runtime count m as `round(week × (1/m) - (m - 1) / (2m))`, reading both constants through the one-hot of m. The quotient's fractional part is a multiple of 1/m, so rounding can't give the wrong integer ([loops, other uses](../lab/ui-exploration/guide/sections/loops.md#other-uses)).

Instancing, the per-item half of One-hot indexing, Bounded visibility compaction and Parallel argmax replace loops, so they moved to [patterns.md](patterns.md).
