# Learned gallery

A photo layout drawn by a small network whose coefficients are the program's source cells. For each of 20 slots, features of the slot's position that are fixed while building, i.e. the position and its sine and cosine at 4 frequencies, go through two ReGLU layers of 6 units to the slot's left, top, width and height. Training steps the coefficients with Adam, using gradients from `circuit.backpropagateReference`, until the rectangles match the target rectangles. The program is 6,320 operations in 8 layers with 244 weights. [lab/reusable-layout](../../lab/reusable-layout/README.md) also keeps a network's parameters in source cells, and feeds it features that change at runtime.

From the repo root:

```sh
bun start                          # the demo site, with the page at /learned-gallery/
bun run start:learning             # the page alone, where its links to the other demos don't resolve
bun test tests/learned-gallery     # 41 tests, about 20 s
```

## The page

You edit a sketch of up to 12 rectangles, one per photo: drag a rectangle to move it, drag its corner to resize it, click empty space to add one, or remove the selected one with Delete or the Delete button. The sketch starts from one of three presets, Story (10 rectangles), Cinema (9) and Contact sheet (11), drawn for a stage that is 5 wide and 4 tall, and each leaves its last cell empty so that a click has room to add a rectangle. Training continues from the current weights while you edit, so a photo follows its rectangle, and the page shows the loss, the step count and a chart of the loss under the photos. A preset and the Retrain button start over from the seeded weights. A delete removes the photo paired with the deleted rectangle, through the optional `outputs` field of the target in trainable-geometry.ts, which marks the outputs in use. The sketch is edited with a mouse or a finger. No key selects or moves a rectangle, so the Tab key doesn't stop at the sketch.

Training runs 600 steps a second at any frame rate: 10 steps in a frame at 60 Hz and 5 at 120 Hz, at most 20 steps per frame, and at most as many as fit in 6 ms at the measured cost of a step, but 2 steps or none, see "A frame trains 2 steps or more, or none" below. After a pause, e.g. while the tab was in the background, every frame trains up to 20 steps until training has caught up with the time or stops. A preset's first fit takes about 1 to 3 s: 630 to 750 steps for Story, 1,360 to 1,840 for Cinema and 860 to 980 for Contact sheet, depending on which steps each frame holds.

## Lining the photos up

The loss has a second part, a misalignment penalty, which pulls photo edges that are nearly on one line onto one line, so the photos line up although the sketch is drawn by hand. Nothing straightens the sketch with ordinary code: training has to lower the penalty. The "Misalignment penalty" slider sets how far that pull reaches. Px in this section are on a stage of 538 by 430 px, except where another stage is named. The page's stage in a window of 1440 px is 620 by 496 px, so 1% of its width is 6.2 px instead of 5.4.

- **The loss** is the sum of two parts, both means over the rectangles and their four edges. The first is the distance from the sketch: the Huber loss of each photo's left, top, right and bottom edge against its rectangle's. The second is the misalignment penalty, which reads only the photos and never the sketch: every two photo edges of one kind, e.g. two tops, are charged for the gap between them. A pair's pull, i.e. the slope of its charge, is 5 times the gap and at most the reach minus the gap, so it is strongest between edges that are nearly in line, and 0 from a gap of one reach on. A pair's charge is divided by the number of edges on the two edges' lines, so a row of 4 holds a photo as far as a row of 2 does. Ordinary code counts the lines from the photos' edges at the start of each frame.
- **The multipliers.** The slope of a cost alone leaves a held photo where its two pulls balance, a fraction of a px beside its line. So ordinary code keeps one more number per kind of edge and pair of photos, grows it by a tenth of the pair's gap per training step, and adds it to the pair's pull, within the same limit (the method of multipliers). A gap that stays open is pulled harder and harder until it closes. The multipliers aren't part of the loss: the loss that the page shows is the cost without them.
- **The slider** "Misalignment penalty" goes from off to 8% and starts at 4%. Its number is the distance at which a photo lets go of its line, as a share of the stage's width, and the penalty's reach is half of it. The penalty counts twice as much as the distance from the sketch, and with that weight the other edges of a line hold a photo until its rectangle is 2 reaches from the line. E.g. on the page in Chrome, on a stage of 620 px, a photo of Story's lower row whose rectangle was dragged slowly let go when the rectangle was 24 to 27 px from the row, where 4% of that stage is 24.8 px. With the slider at off the loss is its first part alone.
- **A photo joins a line at half the distance at which it leaves one,** so the same sketch can end in two layouts, depending on what came before. E.g. a rectangle dragged 14 px out of its row keeps its photo in the row, and after the slider went to off and back to 4%, the photo is on its rectangle.
- **Photos meet in the middle.** The penalty pulls both edges of a pair equally, so a line rests at the mean of where its rectangles are drawn. E.g. when one rectangle of a row of 4 is dragged 12 px down, its photo stays in the row, 9 px above its rectangle, and the row's other photos move 3 px down. A line of two meets halfway: when Story's upper right rectangle is dragged 10 px down, the large photo's top moves 5 px down with it. So the other photos of a line move when one rectangle is dragged, by up to a quarter of the slider's distance in a row of 4 and up to half of it in a line of two, and back when the dragged photo lets go.
- **After a preset or Retrain** the penalty is off until the photos are close to the sketch, i.e. until the distance from the sketch is below 1e-6, at most for 1,000 steps, and then it rises over 200 steps. The seeded weights put the photos in a pile, where every edge is within the reach of every other, so the penalty waits until the photos are where the sketch has them. For the presets it comes on after 150 to 180 steps.
- **The weight decay** is 0.000_000_2. It is added to the gradient, where Adam gives it as much say as a small gradient of the loss, so a decay of 0.000_02 holds the photos 1.4 to 1.8 px from the sketch. Without any decay, units whose gate has closed stay shut: over 200 random edits in a row, 2 to 4 of the first layer's 6 units ended shut in 4 of 6 runs, where with the small decay 2 ended shut in 1 of 12 runs, and 1 or none in the others.
- **The training loop.** Adam at a fixed learning rate doesn't come to rest once the loss is low: its steps keep their size while the gradients shrink, and the photos jump, e.g. by up to 14 px after six small drags in a row. So the page's frames train with `trainOrUndo`: when a frame's steps leave the function that they follow higher than it was before them, the weights and Adam's moments go back, and the learning rate halves, down to 1/64 of its value. That function is the loss with the multipliers in the pulls, and a frame holds the multipliers, the line counts and the penalty's weight constant, so it is one function per frame. A frame that is kept multiplies the rate by 1.15, up to its value. The rate stays as it is when the sketch changes. When 5 frames in a row at 1/64 were taken back, the rate goes on halving, down to 1/4096, and a kept frame doubles it until it is at 1/64 again: without that, a photo whose rectangle was just added far from it can stand still 30 px away, with every frame taken back.
- **A frame trains 2 steps or more, or none.** A frame that is taken back puts Adam's two moments back with the weights, so after a frame of one step the next frame takes the same step at half the rate. Where the momentum itself points uphill, that step raises what it follows at any rate, and every frame is taken back until the multipliers have changed the slopes enough, while the photos stand still. A frame would hold one step on a device where a step takes over 3 ms, since the budget is 6 ms, and on a display of about 500 Hz. The price: where a step takes over 3 ms, a frame's training takes up to twice the 6 ms, e.g. 10 ms at 5 ms a step.
- **When training stops.** With the penalty on, the loss has no value that says "done": it rests where its two parts balance, e.g. at 1e-5 with a photo held 9 px from its rectangle. So the page reads the photos: a change gets at least 500 more steps, and training stops once no photo edge has moved more than 0.01% of the stage from where it was 100 or more steps before, and after 2,000 steps at the latest.

How close the photos end, with the slider at 4%: after a small edit of 5 or 11 px, a line of photos ends 0.01 px apart at the median and 0.16 px at most, and after a preset's first fit within 0.11 px. With the slider at off, the lines of a preset's photos end 0.5 px apart for Story and 0.6 px for Cinema. An edge that is on no line can rest farther from where it is drawn, since the 244 weights rest where the slopes of all edges balance: the top of Story's second photo beside the large one rests 0.6 px from its rectangle, and among 200 variants of Story's proportions, 12 end with such an edge 2 to 4 px off. One training step takes about 0.2 ms in Bun and 0.34 to 0.41 ms in Chrome 155 on an Apple M5 Max.
