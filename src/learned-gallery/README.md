# Learned gallery

A photo layout drawn by a small network whose coefficients are the program's source cells. The network has 20 output slots, of which the page uses up to 12. For each slot, features of the slot's position that are fixed while building, i.e. the position and its sine and cosine at 4 frequencies, go through two ReGLU layers of 6 units to the slot's left, top, width and height. A ReGLU unit is one weighted sum times the ReLU of another ([docs/tutorial.md](../../docs/tutorial.md#builder-code)). Training steps the coefficients with Adam, using gradients from `circuit.backpropagateReference`, until the rectangles match the target rectangles. The program is 6,320 operations in 8 layers. Its 244 source cells are the trained coefficients, which the rest of this README calls the weights.

From the repo root:

```sh
bun start                          # the demo site, with the page at /learned-gallery/
bun run start:learning             # the page alone, where its links to the other demos don't resolve
bun test tests/learned-gallery     # 41 tests, about 20 s
```

## The page

You edit a sketch of up to 12 rectangles, one per photo: drag a rectangle to move it, drag its corner to resize it, click empty space to add one, or remove the selected one with Delete or the Delete button. The sketch starts from one of three presets, Story (10 rectangles), Cinema (9) and Contact sheet (11), drawn for a stage that is 5 wide and 4 tall, and each leaves its last cell empty so that a click has room to add a rectangle. Training continues from the current weights while you edit, so a photo follows its rectangle, and the page shows the loss, the step count and a chart of the loss under the photos. A preset and the Retrain button start over from the seeded weights. Deleting a rectangle removes its photo: the target's optional `outputs` field in trainable-geometry.ts marks which of the network's outputs are in use. The sketch is edited with a mouse or a finger. No key selects or moves a rectangle, so the Tab key doesn't stop at the sketch.

Training runs 600 steps a second at any frame rate, e.g. 10 steps per frame at 60 Hz and 5 at 120 Hz. A frame trains at most 20 steps, at most as many as fit in 6 ms at the measured cost of a step, and never exactly 1: it trains 2 or none, see "A frame trains 2 steps or more, or none" below. After a pause, e.g. while the tab was in the background, every frame trains up to 20 steps until training has caught up with the time or stops. A preset's first fit takes about 1 to 3 s: 630 to 750 steps for Story, 1,360 to 1,840 for Cinema and 860 to 980 for Contact sheet. The count varies from run to run with the timing of the frames.

## Lining the photos up

The loss has a second part, a misalignment penalty, which pulls photo edges that are nearly on one line onto one line, so the photos line up although the sketch is drawn by hand. No code moves a rectangle or a photo onto a line: the photos line up only because training lowers the penalty. The "Misalignment penalty" slider sets how far that pull reaches. The px examples in this section are for a stage 538 px wide, where 4% on the slider, its first value, is 21.5 px, and the penalty's reach is 10.8 px.

- **The loss** is the sum of two parts, both means over the rectangles and their four edges. The first is the distance from the sketch: the Huber loss of each photo's left, top, right and bottom edge against its rectangle's. The second is the misalignment penalty, which reads only the photos and never the sketch: every two photo edges of one kind, e.g. two tops, are charged for the gap between them. The penalty has a reach, the gap from which it leaves two edges alone. A pair's pull, i.e. the slope of its charge, is 5 times the gap and at most the reach minus the gap, so it is strongest between edges that are nearly in line. A pair's charge is divided by the number of edges on the two edges' lines, so a row of 4 holds a photo as far as a row of 2 does. Ordinary code counts the lines from the photos' edges at the start of each frame.
- **The multipliers.** The slope of a cost alone leaves a held photo where its two pulls balance, a fraction of a px beside its line. So ordinary code keeps one more number per kind of edge and pair of photos, grows it by a tenth of the pair's gap per training step, and adds it to the pair's pull, within the same limit (the method of multipliers). A gap that stays open is pulled harder and harder until it closes. The multipliers aren't part of the loss: the loss that the page shows is the cost without them.
- **The slider** "Misalignment penalty" goes from off to 8% and starts at 4%. Its number is the distance at which a photo lets go of its line, as a share of the stage's width, and the penalty's reach is half of it. The penalty counts twice as much as the distance from the sketch, and with that weight the other edges of a line hold a photo until its rectangle is 2 reaches from the line. With the slider at off the loss is its first part alone.
- **A photo joins a line within one reach and leaves it at two,** so the same sketch can end in two layouts, depending on what came before. E.g. a rectangle dragged 14 px out of its row keeps its photo in the row, and after the slider went to off and back to 4%, the photo is on its rectangle.
- **Photos meet in the middle.** The penalty pulls both edges of a pair equally, so a line rests at the mean of where its rectangles are drawn. E.g. when one rectangle of a row of 4 is dragged 12 px down, its photo stays in the row, 9 px above its rectangle, and the row's other photos move 3 px down. A line of two meets halfway: when Story's upper right rectangle is dragged 10 px down, the large photo's top moves 5 px down with it. So the other photos of a line move when one rectangle is dragged, by up to a quarter of the slider's distance in a row of 4 and up to half of it in a line of two, and back when the dragged photo lets go.
- **After a preset or Retrain** the penalty is off until the photos are close to the sketch, i.e. until the distance from the sketch is below 1e-6, at most for 1,000 steps, and then it rises over 200 steps. The seeded weights put the photos in a pile, where every edge is within the reach of every other, so the penalty waits until the photos are where the sketch has them. For the presets it comes on after 150 to 180 steps.
- **The weight decay** is 0.000_000_2. It is added to the gradient, where Adam gives it as much say as an equally small gradient of the loss, so a larger decay holds the photos off the sketch, e.g. by 1.4 to 1.8 px at 0.000_02. Without any decay, a unit whose gate has closed gets no gradient and stays shut: over 200 random edits in a row, 2 to 4 of the first layer's 6 units can end shut, where with this decay at most 2 do, and mostly 1 or none.
- **The training loop.** Adam at a fixed learning rate doesn't come to rest once the loss is low: its steps keep their size while the gradients shrink, and the photos jump, e.g. by up to 14 px after six small drags in a row. So the page's frames train with `trainOrUndo`. A frame holds the multipliers, the line counts and the penalty's weight constant, so its steps follow one function: the loss with the multipliers in the pulls. When that function is higher after a frame's steps than before them, the frame is taken back: the weights and Adam's moments go back, and the learning rate halves, down to 1/64 of the full rate. A frame that is kept multiplies the rate by 1.15, up to the full rate. The rate stays as it is when the sketch changes. When 5 frames in a row at 1/64 were taken back, the rate goes on halving, down to 1/4096, and a kept frame doubles it until it is at 1/64 again: without that, a photo whose rectangle was just added far from it can stand still 30 px away, with every frame taken back.
- **A frame trains 2 steps or more, or none.** A frame that is taken back puts Adam's two moments back with the weights, so after a frame of one step the next frame takes the same step at half the rate. Where the momentum itself points uphill, that step raises what it follows at any rate, and every frame is taken back until the multipliers have changed the slopes enough, while the photos stand still. A frame would hold one step on a device where a step takes over 3 ms, since the budget is 6 ms, and on a display of about 500 Hz. The price: where a step takes over 3 ms, a frame's training takes up to twice the 6 ms, e.g. 10 ms at 5 ms a step.
- **When training stops.** With the penalty on, the loss has no value that says "done": it rests where its two parts balance, e.g. at 1e-5 with a photo held 9 px from its rectangle. So the page reads the photos: a change gets at least 500 more steps, and training stops once no photo edge has moved more than 0.01% of the stage from where it was 100 or more steps before, and after 2,000 steps at the latest.

How close the photos end, with the slider at 4%: after a preset's first fit or one small edit, a line of photos ends at most 0.16 px apart, 0.01 px at the median of the edits. With the slider at off, a preset's lines end farther apart, e.g. 0.5 px for Story and 0.6 px for Cinema. An edge that is on no line can rest farther from where it is drawn, since the edges share the 244 weights, which rest where the slopes of all edges balance: one edge of Story rests 0.6 px from its rectangle, and among 200 variants of Story's proportions, 12 end with such an edge 2 to 4 px off. One training step takes about 0.2 ms in Bun and 0.34 to 0.41 ms in Chrome 155 on an Apple M5 Max.
