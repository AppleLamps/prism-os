More detail

The loss has two parts, and the chart draws both. The first, in copper, is how far each photo’s four edges are from its rectangle’s. The second, in indigo, is the misalignment penalty, which charges two photo edges of one kind, e.g. two tops, for the gap between them. The penalty reaches half as far as the slider says: at 4%, edges closer than 2% of the stage’s width end on one line, between the places where their rectangles are drawn. Each training step changes the weights a little to lower the loss. Ordinary code also keeps a pull for each such pair of edges that grows while their gap stays open, so a photo ends on its line and not beside it, and steps that raise the loss with those pulls counted in are taken back. A photo on a line stays there until its rectangle is about as far away as the slider says, twice as far as a photo joins a line, so the same sketch can end in two layouts. Each change gets at least more steps, and training stops once no photo has moved for steps, or after . Training runs at up to steps a second, and one step takes about on this device. The program is operations in layers, and the weights are its only input.
