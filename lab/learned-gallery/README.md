# Learned gallery

A photo layout drawn by a small network whose coefficients are the program's source cells. For each of 20 slots, features of the slot's position that are fixed while building, i.e. the position and its sine and cosine at 4 frequencies, go through two ReGLU layers of 6 units to the slot's left, top, width and height. Training steps the coefficients with Adam, using gradients from `circuit.backpropagateReference`, until the rectangles match the target rectangles. On the page you edit a sketch of up to 12 rectangles, one per photo: drag a rectangle to move it, drag its corner to resize it, click empty space to add one, or remove the selected one with Delete or the Delete button. Training continues while you edit, so a photo follows its rectangle, and the page shows the loss, the step count and a chart of the loss under the photos. The loss has a second part, a misalignment penalty, which pulls photo edges that are nearly on one line onto one line, so the photos line up although the sketch is drawn by hand. The "Misalignment penalty" slider sets how far that pull reaches.

It moved here from the repo root on September 16, 2026, since the repo now centers on HandwiredOS and its runtime. [lab/reusable-layout](../reusable-layout/README.md) also keeps a network's parameters in source cells, and feeds it features that change at runtime.

October 13, 2026: the page was rebuilt as one of the demos that `bun start` serves. The sketch starts from one of three presets, Story (10 rectangles), Cinema (9) and Contact sheet (11), drawn for a stage that is 5 wide and 4 tall, and each leaves its last cell empty so that a click has room to add a rectangle. An edit continues training from the current weights, where the page before stopped training during a drag and reset the weights at the release. A preset and the Retrain button start over from the seeded weights. Training runs 600 steps a second at any frame rate: 10 steps in a frame at 60 Hz and 5 at 120 Hz, at most 20 steps per frame, and at most as many as fit in 6 ms at the measured cost of a step. A delete removes the photo paired with the deleted rectangle, through the optional `outputs` field of the target in trainable-geometry.ts, which marks the outputs in use. The sketch is edited with a mouse or a finger. No key selects or moves a rectangle, so the Tab key doesn't stop at the sketch. The network is the one from before the rebuild: the program is 6,320 operations in 8 layers with 244 weights. The loss and the training loop changed on October 13 and 14, see the next section.

## Lining the photos up

Cheng asked for "a bigger penalty or adjustable one for learned gallery for grid being unaligned". The first answer, on October 13, 2026, straightened a copy of the sketch with ordinary code before training and fitted the photos to the copy. Cheng: "the spirit of the gallery is to learn something. Snapping to some alignemtn is just a simple thought." Since October 14 nothing straightens the sketch: the photos are lined up by a part of the loss, which training has to lower. Px in this section are on a stage of 538 by 430 px, the page's stage at a 1440 px window until that day. The stage is 620 by 496 px there now, so 1% of its width is 6.2 px instead of 5.4.

- **The loss** is the sum of two parts, both means over the rectangles and their four edges. The first is the distance from the sketch: the Huber loss of each photo's left, top, right and bottom edge against its rectangle's. The second is the misalignment penalty, which reads only the photos and never the sketch: every two photo edges of one kind, e.g. two tops, are charged for the gap between them. A pair's pull, i.e. the slope of its charge, is 5 times the gap and at most the reach minus the gap, so it is strongest between edges that are nearly in line, and 0 from a gap of one reach on. A pair's charge is divided by the number of edges on the two edges' lines, so a row of 4 holds a photo as far as a row of 2 does. Ordinary code counts the lines from the photos' edges at the start of each frame.
- **The multipliers.** The slope of a cost alone leaves a held photo where its two pulls balance, a fraction of a px beside its line. So ordinary code keeps one more number per kind of edge and pair of photos, grows it by a tenth of the pair's gap per training step, and adds it to the pair's pull, within the same limit (the method of multipliers). A gap that stays open is pulled harder and harder until it closes. The multipliers aren't part of the loss: the loss that the page shows is the cost without them.
- **The slider** "Misalignment penalty" goes from off to 8% and starts at 4%. Its number is the distance at which a photo lets go of its line, as a share of the stage's width, and the penalty's reach is half of it. The penalty counts twice as much as the distance from the sketch, and with that weight the other edges of a line hold a photo until its rectangle is 2 reaches from the line. On the page in Chrome, a photo of Story's lower row whose rectangle was dragged slowly let go when the rectangle was 24 to 27 px from the row, where 4% of that stage is 24.8 px, and with the slider at 2%, 6% and 8% it let go between 12 and 13.5 px, 36 and 39 px, and 48 and 52 px. With the slider at off the loss is its first part alone.
- **A photo joins a line at half the distance at which it leaves one,** so the same sketch can end in two layouts, depending on what came before. E.g. a rectangle dragged 14 px out of its row keeps its photo in the row, and after the slider went to off and back to 4%, the photo is on its rectangle. Measured at 4% with every rectangle of the three presets, i.e. the 178 rough drags and resizes of each size that move an edge of a line: a photo that was on its lines stayed there in every edit of 5 and of 11 px, in 89% of the edits of 16 px, in 58% of the edits of 19 px, and at 22 px, the slider's distance, only in the 5 edits whose rectangle the stage's edge stopped after 19 px. A photo that was on its rectangle joined its lines in every edit of 5 px, in 69% of the edits of 11 px, the reach, and in none of the edits of 16 px.
- **Photos meet in the middle.** The penalty pulls both edges of a pair equally, so a line rests at the mean of where its rectangles are drawn. E.g. when one rectangle of a row of 4 is dragged 12 px down, its photo stays in the row, 9 px above its rectangle, and the row's other photos move 3 px down. Charging only the rectangle that was edited later was tried, see below.
- **After a preset or Retrain** the penalty is off until the photos are close to the sketch, i.e. until the distance from the sketch is below 1e-6, at most for 1,000 steps, and then it rises over 200 steps. The seeded weights put the photos in a pile, where every edge is within the reach of every other, so the penalty waits until the photos are where the sketch has them. For the presets it comes on after 150 to 180 steps.
- **The weight decay** has been 0.000_000_2 since October 13, where it was 0.000_02. It is added to the gradient, where Adam gives it as much say as a small gradient of the loss, and it held the photos 1.4 to 1.8 px from the sketch. Without any decay, units whose gate has closed stay shut: over 200 random edits in a row, 2 to 4 of the first layer's 6 units ended shut in 4 of 6 runs. With the small decay, 2 ended shut in 1 of 12 runs, and 1 or none in the others.
- **The training loop.** Adam at a fixed learning rate doesn't come to rest once the loss is low: its steps keep their size while the gradients shrink, and on the page as it was before October 13, six small drags in a row made the photos jump by up to 14 px. Since then the page's frames train with `trainOrUndo`: when a frame's steps leave the function that they follow higher than it was before them, the weights and Adam's moments go back, and the learning rate halves, down to 1/64 of its value. That function is the loss with the multipliers in the pulls, and a frame holds the multipliers, the line counts and the penalty's weight constant, so it is one function per frame. A frame that is kept multiplies the rate by 1.15, up to its value. The rate stays as it is when the sketch changes. When 5 frames in a row at 1/64 were taken back, the rate goes on halving, down to 1/4096, and a kept frame doubles it until it is at 1/64 again: without that, a photo whose rectangle was just added far from it could stand still 30 px away, with every frame taken back.
- **When training stops.** With the penalty on, the loss has no value that says "done": it rests where its two parts balance, e.g. at 1e-5 with a photo held 9 px from its rectangle. So the page reads the photos: a change gets at least 500 more steps, and training stops once no photo edge has moved more than 0.01% of the stage from where it was 100 or more steps before, and after 2,000 steps at the latest.

What was tried and not kept:

- **Straightening the sketch before training** (October 13, the branch overnight/learned-align). `alignRectangles` moved every sketch edge within 3% of a line onto that line, oldest rectangle first, and the loss was the distance from the result. It lined photos up within 0.5 px and moved no other photo, and it wasn't learned: ordinary code decided where every line is, and training only copied the result.
- **Plain forms of the penalty** (the probe of October 14, shares of the edits of 24 sessions of 40 small edits that end with two photo edges 0.5 to 8 px apart): a fixed pull that is rounded near a gap of 0, without multipliers, 78%. The same rounded less, 47%. Multipliers with a limit that stays full for the first 2 px of a gap, 21%. Multipliers with a limit that falls from a gap of 0, the form that is kept, 1.8%. A cost for the gap to the nearest edge only left lines 1.5 to 2 px apart.
- **Charging only the rectangle that was edited later,** so that the older photos stay (the probe, two forms). Single edits were fine: the other photos moved 0.35 px in the middle case. In sessions of small edits, photos still moved over 0.5 px per frame when training stopped, after 37% and 68% of the edits.
- **The slider's number as the penalty's reach itself.** From a reach of 7% on, lines that are apart in a sketch merge: Story has left edges 9.1% apart, and they ended on one line in up to 5 of 18 rough sketches at 7% and 8%. At a reach of 8%, sessions of mixed edits ended with a photo 95 px from its rectangle in the middle case. At a reach of 4%, the slider's top now, none of 162 rough sketches merged two lines, and sessions stay stable, with more edits that end between two layouts than at 2%: 1.7% of mixed edits against 0.5%, and 4.5% of mixed edits train all 2,000 steps against 1.2%.
- **A fixed wait of 200 steps before the penalty** after a preset or Retrain. 2 of the 200 variants of Story ended with one photo 5 to 8 px off its row after 2,000 steps: their fit is slow, the photo was still out of the row's reach when the penalty came on, and the rest of the fit was slower with the penalty on.
- **A learning rate that halves below 1/64 after every frame that is taken back.** It ends the photos that stand still, like the rule of 5 frames, and 1.9% of small session edits ended with two photo edges neither in line nor a reach apart, against 1.1%.
- **The loss before October 13,** a squared pull of each photo edge toward a value shared with the sketch's edges near it: a compromise with the pull toward the rectangle as drawn, so about 1 px remained, and at a strength that closed the rest Cinema didn't find its layout in 500 steps. Squared and absolute pulls between photo edges without a limit that falls to 0 slowed the first fit or left edges that are a few percent apart pulled part of the way.
- **For the jumps of plain Adam:** a learning rate that falls over an edit's steps, AMSGrad, a larger epsilon and a slower second moment, which slow the first fit, make the photos jump when the rate returns, or only delay the jumps.
