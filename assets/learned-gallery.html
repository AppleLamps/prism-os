What’s happening

A tiny neural net places the photos. It has adjustable numbers, called weights, and every photo’s position and size is computed from them. When you edit the sketch, the page nudges the weights about times a second until the photos land where you drew them. That’s training, and it runs live in this tab: one step takes about on this device.

The loss

Training needs a score to shrink, called the loss. The chart draws its two parts: how far the photos are from the sketch, in copper, and how much edges that nearly line up miss each other, in indigo. Every step changes the weights a little so that the score drops. Pairs of edges that stay apart get pulled harder over time, so they end exactly on one line.

The slider

The misalignment penalty sets how tidy the result is. At 4%, two edges of one kind, e.g. two tops, that are closer than 2% of the stage’s width end on one line, even when the sketch is a bit off. Slide it to off, and the photos simply copy the sketch.
